PASS if the text fits every field, team size and depth of work in the universe's scope, or it is a note that names the part of the scope it is for and holds within that part. Judge the text's own words as a careful reader takes them; the id and other fields given with it only help you read it, and their wording does not count for or against it. A text whose own words name a method, document type or part of the universe other than the thing being defined, as where it applies, is a note for that part and may assume what that part is about, but nothing narrower. A question, actor or timing may assume what the thing it belongs to is about, but nothing narrower. Naming the thing being defined or another part of the same universe, or saying how the two relate or differ, is not an assumption, and a question that asks whether something holds does not assume it. Advice is judged by what its words assume, not by whether every kind of work needs it; it fails only when its words assume one product, trade, audience, size of work, depth of work or kind of outcome. A single example that shows what a general definition means is an illustration and does not narrow the text. A list is judged as a whole: it passes when its items together reach across the scope, even if one item is worded in terms of one field, and it does not have to name every possible kind.
FAIL if a question, actor or timing fits only part of what it belongs to; if a text that names no part of the scope as its condition uses wording that assumes one product, trade, audience, size of work or kind of outcome; if it presents one field's habit as what most work does; or if it lists several items or examples and all of them come from one field or one end of the scope, so some part of the scope fits none of them.
