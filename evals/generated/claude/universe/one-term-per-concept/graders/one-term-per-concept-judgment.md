type: llm
focus: last_message
---

PASS if no reader of the text would think a second concept exists where there is one, or think two concepts are one: every defined term of the universe that the text uses keeps its defined meaning, and the text sets up no second name for a concept that already has a term, in the universe or earlier in the text. Words in their plain everyday sense, descriptive phrases, figures of speech whose meaning is plain, shortened forms that plainly point back to a term, a second name the text itself says is the same thing, names for a kind or part of a concept, and different names for things the text keeps apart on purpose all pass, because none of them makes a reader think a second concept exists.
FAIL if the text gives a concept that already has a term, in the universe or in the text, a second name that a reader could take for a separate concept, or if it uses one term for two different concepts, including using a defined term of the universe to mean something other than its defined meaning.
