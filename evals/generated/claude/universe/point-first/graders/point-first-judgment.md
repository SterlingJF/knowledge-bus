type: llm
focus: last_message
---

PASS if the opening words already state a point the text makes, so a reader who stops there has it. That opening may be a claim, a judgment, an instruction, or a statement that names what the text is about and says what it is or does, and everything after it, whether a later clause, a list, a colon-led detail or a later sentence, may explain, qualify, extend or act on it. A text whose opening sentence makes a claim of its own passes even when a later sentence gives an instruction or a further point that follows from it, and a short phrase that only says which case or stage the point covers is not a lead-in.
FAIL if a reader who stops after the opening words has not yet been told any point the text makes, because the text opens with a tag that says what kind of statement follows, a clause that concedes something or gives a reason before the main clause, or background, such as a scene-setting fact or a plain description, from which a later sentence then draws the point.
