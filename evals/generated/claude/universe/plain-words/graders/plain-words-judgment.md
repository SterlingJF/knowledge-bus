PASS if a semi-technical reader who has never seen the file format, and recalls nothing from earlier text, grasps the text on first read and can apply it to their own case. Every word is a term the universe defines or a common word in the sense people use every day for this kind of subject, with no picture of something else behind it, such as drives meaning causes, covers meaning includes, or a support that carries weight. A word that is a little formal still passes when it has one clear literal meaning that this reader knows from daily life, and any effect the text names is one the reader can picture.
FAIL if the reader must stop and work out what a word or phrase means before applying the text. That happens when the text uses a metaphor, an idiom, an analogy or a quotable saying, meaning a word that asks the reader to picture something else, such as a body part, a pulse, waves spreading or the narrow neck of a bottle, even when the phrase is common; when it uses a specialist, formal or abstract word that the reader cannot work out from daily life or could read more than one way, where an everyday word exists, such as naming the length of time a building lasts where preventing mold is meant; or when it needs the file format's own vocabulary, such as a field or section named without saying what it holds.
