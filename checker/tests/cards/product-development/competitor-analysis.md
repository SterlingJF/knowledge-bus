# Competitor analysis

Document type in the Product development universe spec (product-development 0.9).

- Also called: competitor analysis
- Helps you: find where the offering can differ from the other options people have, including doing nothing
- Who uses it: whoever decides what to offer or how to present it
- When it is used: whenever another option appears or changes
- Not used when uptake is given. Depends on: How do people come to take up the offering? (uptake)
- No document needed when time-separation is none and reversibility is reversible. Depends on: Who has to reconstruct this reasoning without the author present? (time-separation); Can this step be undone, and at what cost? (reversibility)

## How to write it well

- Boundary: Distinct from a sales battlecard, which is written from this analysis to win deals. Ask whether an entry reports how well an alternative serves people or argues for the offering; arguments belong on the battlecard.

## Sections

### Competitor profile

Answers: How well does each alternative serve the people the offering is for?
Required.
Applies only when uptake is chosen or bought. Depends on: How do people come to take up the offering? (uptake)

- Heuristic: Per alternative: what it offers, segment overlap, how well it serves those people, pricing where knowable.
- Pitfall: Include indirect alternatives and doing nothing. The spreadsheet-and-email incumbent beats most challengers.
- Boundary: Sales battlecards are the externalised descendant. Keep evidence separate from spin.

### Positioning statement

Answers: Which kind of offering should each segment see ours as?
When applicable.
Applies only when uptake is chosen or bought. Depends on: How do people come to take up the offering? (uptake)

- Convention: For [segment] who [need], X is a [category] that [benefit], unlike [alternative].
- Pitfall: Brand-led organisations treat this as marketing's asset and product-led ones as product's. Settle ownership before it gets written twice.
- Empty, when maturity is unproven: Pre-competition work often cannot fill the 'unlike' clause honestly. Leave it empty rather than fake it. Depends on: How settled is the bet this work rests on? (maturity)

### External opportunities threats

Answers: Which outside opportunities and threats matter to the work?
When applicable.
Kept in another document (Swot analysis): link to that document. The notes below are for writing the section in that document.

- Boundary: Distinct from Macro-Environment Factors, which lists outside conditions lens by lens; this judges which of them, and which competitor moves, help or threaten the work. The same law may appear in both: once as a fact, once as a threat with an owner.
- Heuristic: Draw each opportunity and threat from the PESTLE scan or the competitor profiles, and say which one it came from.
- Pitfall: A threat with no owner and no trigger gets noticed only once it lands. Carry each threat that needs a response into Risks & Assumptions, where it gets an owner.

## What the conditions mean

- uptake is given: Does it arrive whether or not they'd choose it?
- uptake is chosen: Do they take it up only by deciding to, with nothing to pay?
- uptake is bought: Is paying how they take it up?
