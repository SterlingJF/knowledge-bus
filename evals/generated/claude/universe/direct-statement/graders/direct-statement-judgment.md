PASS if the text states only what holds, every verb is plain, nothing is denied or set against a rejected option, and every action someone takes either names who takes it or is an instruction to the reader. Labels, lists, short fragments and questions pass when they contain none of the faults in the FAIL line. A comparison such as better than states what holds. A question that asks about alternatives rejects none. A condition that names a real case, such as if not, states when something applies. A word that describes a thing, such as measured or observed, needs no actor. A phrase used in its literal sense, such as an oven that turns on, is plain.
FAIL if the text says what something is not, never does or does not need; defines something by what it is distinct from; sets its claim against a rejected option, as with instead of, rather than or a trailing not followed by that option; uses a double negative such as nothing leaves unlabeled; uses a vague verb phrase such as turns on or takes care of where a plain verb exists; or uses a passive clause such as is set or are placed that leaves out who acts, unless the person given an instruction is the one who acts.
